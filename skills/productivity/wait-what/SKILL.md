# Wait, what?

Fire this the instant a message does not land. Do not spend five minutes
re-reading something that was not explained well the first time; ask for a
re-pitch instead.

## What a good re-pitch does

1. **Restates the point in plain language**, without the jargon that caused
   the confusion in the first place. If a term is genuinely load-bearing,
   define it inline in one clause instead of assuming it is known.
2. **Uses the reader's own vocabulary**, not the codebase's internal one, if
   the two differ. Translate, do not just repeat louder.
3. **Leads with the "so what"**: what changes, what the reader needs to do or
   decide, before the supporting detail.
4. **Is shorter than the original**, not longer. A re-pitch that adds more
   words than the first attempt has not actually fixed the problem.

## As the one asking

Say what specifically did not land, if you can, "what does X mean here" beats
a bare "wait, what?" because it saves a round trip. But a bare "wait, what?"
is still a completely valid thing to send. Getting re-explained is always
cheaper than guessing and building the wrong thing.
